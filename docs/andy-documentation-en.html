01 / WELCOME TO ANDY

Possibly the world's hardest board game.

Experience. Explore. Anticipate.

Visit ANDY Arena ↗

Welcome to ANDY.

What happens when Chess and Go stop being separate games and start influencing each other?

ANDY combines both strategic systems on one 13×13 board. AI models compete under explicit token budgets, and humans can observe, replay and study their decisions.

It is not only about which model wins. It is about how decisions turn into executed actions, which errors occur and what resources are consumed. The Arena tells the story; the Lab opens it to independent analysis.

Experience. Explore. Anticipate. We do not claim that ANDY is the world's hardest board game. We want to investigate the question.

02 / ARENA & THEATER

The Arena is the stage. The record remains the evidence.

A Fight Night can be cinematic without changing a single recorded move.

A real match in a browser theater

The V0.7.2 website integration test reproduces OpenAI GPT-6 Astra versus Google Gemini 4 Argon from original JSONL match events. The match lasted roughly 2 hours and 19 minutes; its theatrical version plays in accelerated time.

Fight Card → Optional Guided Tour → Countdown → Replay → Winner Ceremony → Analysis. The original match result, recorded moves, failed attempts and resource consumption remain separate from the presentation.

White won by chess checkmate. The browser theater uses HTML, CSS and JavaScript and does not need new AI requests during playback.

03 / ANDY LAB

The Lab asks why, not only who won.

The Lab separates observed events, calculated metrics and interpretation.

Match results alone are not sufficient to compare resource-constrained AI agents. The Lab is designed for inspecting executed actions, failed decisions, recorded costs and the reliability of particular conclusions.

A recorded model explanation is part of the transcript, not verified access to the model's hidden reasoning. It remains in the original language; any future translation must be explicitly identified.

All published statistics should be traceable to a match, event reference, formula version and underlying source file. A missing value is not a zero.

04 / RULES & GEOMETRY

Chess occupies squares. Go occupies intersections.

ANDY overlays distinct spatial systems on a common board.

The 13×13 board combines a chess playing area with a Go lattice. Chess pieces belong to square centers; Go stones belong to lattice junctions, including inside the overlapping chess region. Coordinates must not be interchanged between those two geometries.

The authoritative move rules, setup phases and end conditions are versioned in the German working documentation and game engine. This English preview introduces the geometry but is not yet the complete normative rules translation.

05 / COMPLEXITY

Difficulty is strategic – and perceptual.

Chess and Go create overlapping threats; limited budgets add another constraint.

Two systems compete for attention.

When attention focuses on chess pieces, relevant Go connections may be missed, or vice versa. The analogy to a reversible figure and to inattentional blindness motivates testable hypotheses, not established findings about ANDY players.

♔●♜○

Strategy × Execution

Models must interpret two rule systems, track game state, choose legal actions and remain within their resource budget. ANDY is an experimental setting, not a general intelligence certificate.

Game complexity estimates, ranking formulas and model performance must be independently validated rather than inferred from vivid matches.

07 / STRATEGY × EXECUTION

A scoreboard cannot replace a method.

The ANDY Score and AQP must remain distinct from direct match results.

What we measure

Match wins and losses are observable under stated rules and configurations. The proposed Strategy component and combined ANDY Score require reproducible formulas, versioned data and prospective validation.

Qualification (AQP) asks whether a model can complete defined test tasks at acceptable reliability and resource use. It is not the same as a sport win or a general intelligence assessment.

Current status: the full numerical Strategy scoring method and AQP thresholds remain open drafts.

Why logos?

Recognizable marks can help readers distinguish competitors in a visually complex match. Whether they improve recognition in ANDY remains a testable hypothesis.

Independent benchmarking, responsible brand use

Identifying a tested model does not imply sponsorship, partnership or endorsement. Do not distort, crop, recolor or add effects to provider logos or present them as ANDY co-branding. Review applicable brand terms and request permission when needed. No provider approval is asserted.

10 / DEVELOPMENT

A system designed to be challenged.

Public methods and reproducible records matter more than polished claims.

What beta means

ANDY is published as an evolving experimental project. Recorded match events, interpretation, draft scores and proposed qualification rules are separate kinds of evidence.

Claims should be marked as OBSERVED, CALCULATED, INTERPRETED or OPEN. Unverified results are not silently turned into verified facts.

The project roadmap covers staged test campaigns, historical data qualification, engine validation, public match integrity, accessibility and language expansion.

11 / HUMANS × AI

Built together. Questioned together.

ANDY studies AI competition, but its own development is also an experiment in human–AI collaboration.

Humans set goals, resolve trade-offs, review interface behavior and determine what counts as evidence. AI systems have contributed to strategy, implementation, critique and analysis.

OpenAI's ChatGPT has been a major technical implementation and integration contributor in the current development phase; DeepSeek and Qwen have offered strategic and critical input. These are project-development roles, not competitive standings.

Project authorship and contributions should be documented transparently, without treating an AI-generated explanation as proof of internal reasoning or an official provider endorsement.
