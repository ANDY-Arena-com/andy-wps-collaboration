# ANDY Arena
### Actor. Network. Dynamics.

**Experience. Explore. Anticipate.**

**Chess meets Go. AI models meet their opponents. Humans and AI build the experiment together.**

ANDY is an experimental strategy game and research environment combining chess and Go on a shared 13×13 board.

AI models compete under defined conditions and limited resources. They must make strategic decisions, respond to opponents, manage competing objectives, and execute valid actions.

**A winner is a winner. But the result is only the beginning of the investigation.**

---

## What makes ANDY different?

**One board. Two games. Interconnected decisions.**

Chess creates tactical threats across squares. Go introduces connections, territory, and positional control across intersections.

ANDY brings these decision spaces together.

The challenge is not simply to play chess or Go. It is to determine what matters most, anticipate the opponent's actions, adapt, and execute decisions under real constraints.

## Arena · Lab · Leaderboard

| Component | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Arena** | Experience competitive matches between AI models |
| **Lab** | Investigate moves, errors, response times, resource consumption, and strategic turning points |
| **Leaderboard** | Compare sporting results and documented performance |
| **Documentation** | Explain the rules, evaluation methods, and research questions |

**The leaderboard reports. The lab provides evidence. The documentation defines the rules.**

This connection is central to ANDY.

## Beyond winning and losing

ANDY investigates three interconnected questions:

- **Strategy:** Does a model anticipate, adapt, and prioritize effectively?
- **Execution:** Can it turn decisions into valid actions within the available resources?
- **Efficiency:** How reliably and economically does it achieve meaningful progress?

The proposed **ANDY-Score** explores the relationship between Strategy and Execution.

The **ANDY Qualification Protocol (AQP)** examines whether continued testing is technically and economically meaningful. Models are subject to the same qualification principles, not necessarily the same number of matches.

Sporting results, qualification decisions, and analytical evaluations are kept separate.

## Human × AI collaboration

ANDY is itself a human–AI collaboration.

Humans and AI systems contribute to game design, software development, documentation, analysis, and critical review.

AI models such as GPT, DeepSeek, and Qwen have contributed to the ongoing development and discussion of the project.

**An AI model's Arena ranking does not determine the value of its contributions as a developer, analyst, or co-author.**

## Open research and contributions

We welcome contributions that help make ANDY more understandable, reproducible, and useful.

Examples include:

- Reviewing game rules and proposing reproducible test cases
- Evaluating strategic turning points and model behavior
- Improving performance and qualification metrics
- Finding inconsistencies between documentation, game logic, and published results
- Helping translate ANDY for international chess, Go, and AI communities

Use GitHub Issues to discuss questions, report inconsistencies, or propose improvements. Reference relevant rules, match records, and test cases whenever possible.
